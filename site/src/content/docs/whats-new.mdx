title: What's new in Squiffy 7
description: The new editor, the desktop app, calculations, and the other changes in Squiffy 7

Squiffy 7 brings a new editor. The language adds the [`@end`](/basic-concepts/#endings) directive for marking endings, [calculations](/helpers-reference/#calculations), and the same rules for `@set` as for `{{#if}}`. Squiffy 6 stories still work, and the editor converts them for you: see [Coming from Squiffy 6](/coming-from-squiffy-6/). See [Using the editor](/editor/) for a tour.

## The new editor

- **Stories in several files.** A story can be split across `.squiffy` files, each in its own tab. New stories have a [`squiffy.json` project file](/multiple-files/), so every `.squiffy` file in the folder is part of the story, and every stylesheet and script, with no `@import`s needed. The command-line tool and the Vite plugin read it too. The command-line tool builds a story with a `squiffy.json` into a `build` folder, away from its own files.
- **[Images and other files](/images-and-files/).** Add images, sounds and fonts to your story, and they're included when you publish it.
- **[Fonts](/styling/#fonts).** Choose a font for your story from over 2,000 open source fonts, including every Google font. It's copied into your story, so it works offline, and the published story doesn't load anything from Google.
- **An editor that understands your story.** Problems such as links to missing sections, sections the player can't reach, and dead ends are underlined as you type, often with a quick fix. Links suggest your section and passage names as you type them. You can go from a link to its section, find every link to a section, and rename a section or passage everywhere at once.
- **A [story map](/story-map/)** shows your sections, passages and links as a map you can move around, drawn from your script as you type. Problems show on it too, as does the way you went through the story when you test it. You can add links and sections, and rename them, from the map.
- **An outline** of your sections and passages, with a search box, replaces the section and passage dropdowns.
- **Run from any section**, so you can test the part you're working on without playing through from the start. As you click through the story, the script follows.
- **Stories in your browser**, without choosing a file, in every browser. Download one as a `.zip` to keep a copy.
- **Folders.** In Chrome and Edge, the editor can open a story's folder on your computer, and remembers the folders you've opened.
- **A dark theme**, and a text size setting. The preview follows it, and has its own light/dark button for checking your story's styles in both.
- **A [desktop app](/desktop-app/)** for Mac, Windows and Linux, with the same editor, which keeps your stories in folders on your computer.
- **Import from Twine.** Open a [Twine](https://twinery.org/) story, written in Harlowe or SugarCube, and the editor converts it to Squiffy. See [Converting a Twine story](/converting-from-twine/).
- **Import from Quest.** Open a gamebook made in [Quest Viva](https://questviva.com) or Quest 5, and the editor converts it to Squiffy. See [Converting a Quest gamebook](/converting-from-quest/).

## The language

- **Calculations.** Work out values with `+`, `-`, `*`, `/` and `%` in conditions, in `{{set}}`, `{{inc}}` and `{{dec}}`, and in the new `{{= ...}}`, which displays the result: `{{set "gold" gold + price * 2}}`, `{{#if gold >= price * 2}}`, or `That costs {{= price * 2}} gold.` There are matching [maths helpers](/helpers-reference/#maths-helpers) too: `add`, `sub`, `mul`, `div` and `mod`. See [Calculating values](/attributes/#calculating-values).
- **Helpers for numbers and text.** Round numbers with `round`, `floor` and `ceil`, find the smallest or largest with `min` and `max`, and change text with `upper`, `lower` and `capitalise`: `{{= (round (gold / 3))}}` or `{{upper name}}`. `contains` checks what's in an array or some text, as in `{{#if bag contains "key"}}` or `{{#if name contains "Mac"}}`, and `.length` counts the items or letters. See [Maths helpers](/helpers-reference/#maths-helpers) and [Text helpers](/helpers-reference/#text-helpers).
- **More for arrays.** Write an array as a list with `join`, as in `You have {{join bag ", " " and "}}.`, put it in order with `sort` or a random order with `shuffle`, and take part of it with `slice`, `first` and `last`. `indexof` and `count` find and count items, and `range` makes a list of numbers, so `{{#each (range 1 lives)}}♥{{/each}}` repeats something a number of times. See [Joining, sorting and more](/helpers-reference/#joining-sorting-and-more).
- **Choosing between two values.** `hp > 0 ? "alive" : "dead"` is one value or the other, so you can use it in `@set` and inside a calculation, where `{{#if}}` can't go: `You have {{= gold}} {{= gold = 1 ? "coin" : "coins"}}.` See [Choosing between two values](/helpers-reference/#choosing-between-two-values).
- **`@set` and link assignments work like `{{#if}}`.** Text goes in quotes and attributes don't need an `@`, so a value can be a calculation or a comparison: `@set weapon = "sword"`, `@set gold = gold + price * 2`, `@set rich = gold > 100`, or `[[Buy]](shop, gold -= price)`. See [Setting attributes](/attributes/#setting-attributes).
- **Go to another section from your text.** [`{{goto "section"}}`](/basic-concepts/#go-to-another-section) moves the story on without a link, for example `{{#if health <= 0}}{{goto "death"}}{{/if}}`, and `{{goto "darkness" after=3000}}` does it after a delay, which can be worked out as the story runs: `after=patience * 1000` or `after="3s"`.
- **Reusable text with parameters.** [`{{embed}}`](/basic-concepts/#parameters) can pass values to the section it shows, so one section can be used all over the story with different words: `{{embed "say" who="Ann" line="Hello!"}}`, which the section shows as `{{who}}` and `{{line}}`. Parameters don't change the story's attributes, and only last while the embed is shown. `{{#embed "speech"}}Halt!{{/embed}}` [wraps some text](/basic-concepts/#wrapping-text), which the section shows with `{{content}}`. When you import a Twine story, its widgets and macros become these.
- **Visit and turn counts.** [`{{visits "cave"}}`](/attributes/#the-visits-helper) says how many times the player has been to a section or passage, for example `{{#if (visits "cave") > 2}}`, and the [`turns` attribute](/attributes/#counting-turns) counts the links clicked in the whole story.
- **Where the player came from.** The [`previous_section` and `previous` attributes](/attributes/#where-the-player-came-from) say which section, and which section or passage, the player saw before this one, so a section can begin differently depending on how they got there: `{{#if previous_section = "river"}}`.
- **Endings.** Mark the sections where your story ends with [`@end`](/basic-concepts/#endings), and the editor won't report them as dead ends.
- **Passages you can click again.** Add [`@repeat`](/basic-concepts/#passages-you-can-click-again) to a passage, and its links stay clickable, for a clue the player might want to read again, or a character who says something different each time.
- **Turning off passage links.** [`{{deactivate "name"}}`](/basic-concepts/#turning-off-passage-links) turns off the links to a passage, so one choice can rule out another, and a passage with `@repeat` can turn itself off when it's done.
- **Loops with links.** Inside [`{{#each}}`](/attributes/#going-through-an-array), you can use the story's attributes, and a link can use the item, so you can make a link for each thing in an array: `{{#each inventory}}[[Drop the {{this}}]](drop, item = "{{this}}"){{/each}}`. A link's text can show attributes anywhere, as in `[[Pay {{price}} gold]](shop)`.
- **Objects.** An attribute can hold an [object](/attributes/#objects), a set of named values, such as `@set hero = { hp: 10, name: "Ann" }`, with `{{hero.hp}}`, `@set hero.hp += 5` and `{{#if hero.hp > 5}}` for the values inside it. Arrays can hold objects, and you can write arrays in square brackets and pick out an item with another attribute: `{{= party[leader].name}}`.
- **Music and sound.** [`@music`](/images-and-files/#music-and-sound) plays music that keeps playing from one section to the next until it's changed, fading from one track to the next, and `@sound` plays a sound effect, which `@sound stop` stops. The Back button and saved games put the music back as it was, and players get a mute button.
- **Changing the look for a scene.** [`@class dark`](/styling/#changing-the-look-for-a-scene) puts a class on the story while the player is in a section, so your CSS can make a scene dark, red or anything else, and it changes back when they move on. `{{addclass}}`, `{{removeclass}}` and `{{toggleclass}}` change classes from the text, and `{{#if (hasclass "dark")}}` checks them. The Back button and saved games put them back as they were.
- **JavaScript after the text.** [`squiffy.ui.afterWrite`](/javascript/#running-javascript-after-the-text) runs some JavaScript once a section or passage's text is on the page, with its `{{...}}` worked out.

## Published stories

- **A Back button.** Players can undo their choices, a section or passage at a time. To leave it out, see [The Back button](/multiple-files/#the-back-button).
- **Panels.** Show the player's stats, inventory, a map or some help beside the story, or from a button in the header, always up to date. See [Panels](/multiple-files/#panels).
- **Saved games.** Players can keep as many saved games as they like, and save them to files, from the Save button. See [Saved games](/multiple-files/#saved-games).
- **Dark mode.** Published stories follow the reader's light or dark system setting, and have a button to switch between them. To keep your story light, see [Light and dark mode](/styling/#light-and-dark-mode).
- **Buttons in your language.** The buttons above the story are icons, and their tooltips can be [in your story's language](/multiple-files/#the-pages-text-in-another-language).
- **A fresher look.** The default style has more space between lines, softer colours, a modern serif font, and text sizes that follow the reader's browser setting. Its colours, fonts and page width are [CSS variables](/styling/#common-changes), so they're easier to change, and a stylesheet you add in the editor starts with the most common changes, ready to uncomment.

## The command-line tool

- **Check a story** with [`--check`](/cli/#checking-a-story), which finds the problems the editor shows, such as links to missing sections, sections the player can't reach and dead ends, without building anything.
- **Play a story as text** with [`--play`](/cli/#playing-a-story-from-the-command-line), clicking links with `--choose` and filling in text boxes with `--input`, and carry on from where you left off with `--save`.
- **Play at random** with [`--random`](/cli/#playing-at-random), which plays a story many times to find errors and dead ends, and the sections it never got to, with the options that get back to each problem.
- **For AI agents.** Add `--json` to any of these for a result that a program or an AI agent, such as Claude Code, can read, so an agent helping you write a story can check it and play it. See [For AI agents](/cli/#for-ai-agents).

## Coming from an earlier version

- **Squiffy 6:** your stories still work. See [Coming from Squiffy 6](/coming-from-squiffy-6/) for what's changed, and how the editor and the command-line tool convert a story to Squiffy 7's rules.
- **Squiffy 5:** Squiffy 6 was a complete rewrite, so there's more that's new. The text processor, for things like `{if ...}`, changed to [Handlebars](https://handlebarsjs.com/), and Squiffy can convert most of a story for you. The editor no longer needs a textadventures.co.uk account. Squiffy 6 also added [animation](/dynamic-text/#animation), [live text](/dynamic-text/#live), [player input](/player-input/) and single-file publishing. See [Coming from Squiffy 5](/coming-from-squiffy-5/).
