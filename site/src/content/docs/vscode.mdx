If you'd rather write in [Visual Studio Code](https://code.visualstudio.com/), Squiffy has an extension for it. It understands your story as the [editor](/editor/) does, spotting problems as you type, and runs your story beside your script as you write.

<Screenshot name="vscode" alt="VS Code, with a story's script in the editor, its sections in the Outline, and the story running in the Squiffy Preview beside it" />

## Installing the extension

In VS Code, open the **Extensions** view, search for **Squiffy**, and choose **Install**. Or install it from its [page on the Visual Studio Marketplace](https://marketplace.visualstudio.com/items?itemName=textadventures.squiffy).

It works in VS Code 1.91 or later, on Mac, Windows and Linux, and in VS Code in your browser at [vscode.dev](https://vscode.dev) and github.dev.

## Starting a story

The extension has a getting-started walkthrough on VS Code's **Welcome** page, which takes you through making a story, playing it, adding sections and publishing it. To open it, choose **Help > Welcome**, or **Welcome: Open Walkthrough...** from the Command Palette, and pick **Get Started with Squiffy**.

To start a new story, open an empty folder with **File > Open Folder...**, then click the **Sq.** button in the Activity Bar at the side of the window and choose **New Story**, or choose **Squiffy: New Story** from the Command Palette (**Ctrl+Shift+P**, or **Cmd+Shift+P** on a Mac). Or right-click a folder in the Explorer and choose **New Squiffy Story Here**. It makes a [`squiffy.json` project file](/project-file/) and a `story.squiffy` to start from.

To bring in a story from Twine, choose **Convert Twine Story** in the sidebar, or **Squiffy: Convert Twine Story** from the Command Palette, and pick the Twine file: a `.twee` file, or Twine's published or archive HTML. For a gamebook from Quest, choose **Convert Quest Gamebook** and pick the `.quest` or `.aslx` file. Then choose a folder for the converted story. If the folder isn't empty, the story goes in a new folder in it, named after the file. Its main file opens, and `twine-import.txt` or `quest-import.txt` lists anything that couldn't be converted exactly. See [Converting from Twine](/converting-from-twine/) and [Converting from Quest](/converting-from-quest/).

To work on a story you already have, open its folder. A story you made in the editor can be downloaded with **File > Download project (.zip)** and unzipped, and a folder made by the [desktop app](/desktop-app/) opens as it is.

A folder with a `squiffy.json` is a story, made of every `.squiffy`, `.css` and `.js` file in it, as the editor and the [command-line tool](/cli/) see it. A `.squiffy` file anywhere else is part of the story in its own folder, whose main file is the first one you open there (with the files it [imports](/multiple-files/)). **Squiffy: Use as Main File** picks another.

## The Squiffy sidebar

The **Sq.** button in the Activity Bar opens the Squiffy sidebar. Its **Sections** view lists every section in your story, with its passages under it, across all of its files: if there's more than one, they're grouped by file, the main file first. It's updated as you type, and the section the cursor is in is selected as you move around your script.

Each one is marked as on the [story map](#the-story-map): where the story starts, its endings, dead ends, sections that can't be reached, sections for [testing](/testing/), and sections that are only shown inside others. Hover over one to see what its marks mean.

- Click a section or passage to go to it in your script.
- Its ▷ button, or **Preview from Here** in its right-click menu, starts the preview there.
- The buttons at the top of the view run the preview, add a section, show the story map, and export the story.

If your workspace has more than one story, the view shows the one you're working on.

## Writing

- **Problems as you type**, for the whole story, in the **Problems** panel and underlined in your script: links to sections that don't exist, sections nothing links to, dead ends, and the rest of what the editor reports. Many have a quick fix (the light bulb, or **Ctrl+.**). A warning you don't want can be [turned off](/cli/#turning-off-a-warning).
- **Completion** of section and passage names in links, attributes and directives, with a description when you hover over one.
- **Go to Definition** goes from a link to its section or passage, **Find All References** lists every link to one, and **Rename Symbol** (**F2**) renames it everywhere at once.
- The **Outline** view and breadcrumbs list the sections and passages in a file, and the [Squiffy sidebar](#the-squiffy-sidebar) lists the whole story's.
- Above each section and passage, how many links lead to it: click it to see them.
- **Squiffy: Add Section** and **Add Passage** add a header at the cursor to type a name into. They're also in the right-click menu, and at the top right of a `.squiffy` file: the **+** button adds a section, and the **...** menu beside it has Add Passage.
- The editor's [snippets](/snippets/#built-in-snippets) are offered as you type, such as `if` for an `{{#if}}` block.
- Syntax highlighting and folding of sections and passages.

## Playing your story

**Squiffy: Show Preview**, or the ▷ button at the top right of a `.squiffy` file or the story's `squiffy.json`, or **Preview** in the status bar at the bottom, runs your story beside your script, updating as you type. The preview's own buttons go back a step and restart the story.

To try a part of the story without playing through from the start, click **Preview from here** above a section or passage, or right-click in one and choose **Start Preview at This Section**. See [Testing your story](/testing/) for more ways to test. The attributes the story sets as you play are listed in the **Output** panel: choose **Squiffy Preview** from the dropdown at its top right.

## The story map

**Squiffy: Show Story Map**, or the map button at the top right of a `.squiffy` file or `squiffy.json`, shows the [story map](/story-map/) beside your script: your sections as boxes, their passages under them, and the links between them as arrows, with dead ends, broken links and sections nothing leads to marked. It's redrawn as you type, and it follows VS Code's light or dark theme.

<Screenshot name="vscode-story-map" alt="VS Code, with the game show's script on the left and its story map on the right, zoomed in on the section the cursor is in, play the round, which is selected, with arrows to the rounds it leads to. A panel along the bottom of the map shows the section's buttons and the sections it links to and from." />

- **Click** a box to go to it in your script, or **double-click** it (or select it and press **Enter**) to go there and start typing. A broken link's red box goes to the link.
- The box for the section the cursor is in is highlighted.
- **Hover over** a box to see the start of its text, and what its marks mean.
- The selected box's details are shown at the bottom of the map: the start of its text, whether the story starts or ends there, or can't be reached, buttons to show it in your script, rename it, add a passage, run the preview from it, change its box's size or delete it (and, for a dead end, mark it as an ending, with `@end`), and the sections it links to and is linked from. Click one of those to select it.
- **Drag** a box to move it. Where you put it is saved in `squiffy.json`, as the editor saves it, so the editor's map and VS Code's look the same.
- **Zoom in** to see the start of each section's text on its box, or make a box bigger with **Size…** in its details, or by right-clicking it: **Box: wide**, **Box: tall** or **Box: large**, which is saved in `squiffy.json` too, as in the [editor](/story-map/).
- To **add a link**, click a box, then drag the dot under it to another box. The link is added to your script, at the cursor if it's in that section, and **Undo** takes it out again.
- **Right-click** a box (or select it and press **Shift+F10**) for a menu: **Rename** it and the links to it (or press **F2**), **Add a passage** to it with a link to it, **Run from here** in the preview, **Start the story here**, or **Delete** it (or press **Delete**), with the links to it that are on lines of their own. **Undo** takes out any of these changes to your script.

- To **add a section**, click **Add a section**, double-click an empty part of the map, or right-click it and choose **Add a section here**, and give it a name. It goes in your script after the selected section, or the one nearest on the map. Drag the dot under a box to empty space instead to add a section with a link to it.
- To **search** the map, type in the box at its top right, or press **Ctrl+F** (**Cmd+F** on a Mac) while the map has the focus. It finds sections and passages by their names and by what's in them, as the editor's map does, and dims the rest. **Enter** selects each match in turn (**Shift+Enter** goes back), and **Escape** clears the search.
- A broken link's red box can make what it's missing: double-click it, or right-click it and choose **Add this section** (or **Add this passage**).

- While you play the story in the **Squiffy Preview**, the sections and passages you've been to are outlined in green on the map, and so are the links you took between them. Starting the story again clears them.
- **Key** explains the map's colours, lines and badges. To put every box back where the map would put it, right-click an empty part of the map and choose **Lay out again**.

## Exporting

**Squiffy: Export** builds the playable story, as the editor and the command-line tool build it, to [publish](/publishing/) on a website. Choose:

- a **folder**, with `index.html`, `story.js`, the page's stylesheets and your story's images, sounds and other files
- a **zip** of the same files, to upload to a site such as itch.io or textadventures.co.uk
- a **single HTML file** with everything in it
- only **`story.js`**, to [embed](/embedding/) in a page of your own

## Squiffy 5 stories

**Squiffy: Convert from Squiffy 5** converts the Squiffy 5 text processor syntax in the file you have open, and lists anything it can't convert in the **Problems** panel. It doesn't change how the story sets attributes to Squiffy 7's rules: for that, use [the command-line tool](/cli/#converting-from-squiffy-5). See [Coming from Squiffy 5](/coming-from-squiffy-5/#converting-a-story).
