In this tutorial, you'll write a short story about a castaway who finds a mysterious island. Along the way, you'll use everything you need for most Squiffy stories: sections and passages, remembering what the player has done, choices that depend on it, endings, and a little atmosphere. Then you'll publish it, ready to share.

It takes about half an hour. You don't need to know any programming.

## Start a new story

Open the editor at [app.squiffystory.com](https://app.squiffystory.com/), or the [desktop app](/desktop-app/), and choose **New story**. The desktop app asks for the story's name first: call it "The Island".

The new story has some text in it already, to get you started. Select all of it (Ctrl+A, or Cmd+A on Mac) and delete it: you're going to write your own.

You'll see three columns: the files and outline of your story on the left, the script you write in the middle, and the story itself on the right, which updates as you type. If you can't see the story, press **Run** (Ctrl+Enter, or Cmd+Enter on Mac).

<Aside type="tip">
Every step of this tutorial has the story so far, which you can play on the right and edit on the left. You can copy it into the editor, but you'll learn more by typing in the changes yourself.
</Aside>

## Write the opening

A Squiffy story is made of **sections**. Each one is some text, and the player moves from one section to the next by clicking a link.

Type this into the editor:

<Sample script={step1}></Sample>

Here's what's happening:

- `@title` sets the story's title, which appears in the browser tab.
- The text at the top, before any section name, is where the story starts.
- `[[row towards the island]]` is a link to a section called "row towards the island". Double square brackets mean "go to a section".
- `[[row towards the island]]:`, on a line of its own and with a colon at the end, starts that section. Everything after it, up to the next section, is the section's text.

Play it, and choose a link. Once you have, the other link stops working. That's how sections work: each choice moves the story on. To choose again, the player can use **Back**, at the top of the story, which undoes their last choice. In the editor, **Back** is next to the story.

## Let the player look around

Not everything the player does needs to move the story on. On the beach, they should be able to look at things in any order before they choose where to go next. That's what **passages** are for.

Add some passages to the beach, and a path for the player to follow:

<Sample script={step2}></Sample>

- `[footprints]`, in single square brackets, is a link to a passage. `[footprints]:` starts the passage, in the same way as a section.
- A passage's text appears under the section it belongs to, and the section's other links still work. So the player can look at the footprints and the hut, in either order, before following the path.
- Passages belong to the section above them, so put each one after its section and before the next section.

As soon as the player follows the path, any passages they didn't look at stop working, just as the other section links do.

Try **Outline**, on the left of the editor: it lists your sections and passages, so you can jump to any of them. **Code + Map** in the toolbar shows them as a [story map](/story-map/) too.

## Remember what the player has done

The hut has a lantern in it. Let the player pick it up, and let the story remember that they did, with an **attribute**:

<Sample script={step3}></Sample>

- `[lantern]` is a passage link inside another passage. Passages can link to more passages.
- `@set has_lantern`, on a line of its own, sets an attribute called `has_lantern` when the passage is shown. An attribute is something the story remembers, such as whether the player has the lantern, their name, or their score. You can call it anything you like, as long as it's one word (use `_` instead of spaces).
- `{{#if has_lantern}}...{{/if}}` only shows the text inside it if `has_lantern` is set. So whether the player sees the lantern on the path depends on whether they picked it up.

Play it twice: once taking the lantern, and once without.

Below the story in the editor, **Attributes** shows the values your story has set, which is useful for checking that things are working.

## Make choices matter

Now give the player somewhere to use the lantern. Add a cave, where what happens depends on whether they have it:

<Sample script={step4}></Sample>

- `[[Go into the cave]](cave)` shows the text "Go into the cave", and goes to the section called "cave". Use this when you want the link text to be different from the section's name.
- `{{else}}` shows different text when the condition isn't true. With the lantern, the player finds someone in the cave. Without it, they run away.
- Each of these has its own link, so the player's choice on the beach decides which way the story goes.

Look at the sections for the endings in the editor. Each one is underlined: the editor warns that the player has no way out. That's because nothing tells it these are endings, rather than sections you haven't finished yet.

## Add endings

To tell Squiffy that a section is an ending, put `@end` on a line of its own at the start of it. And give the player a way to play again, with a `{{restart}}` link:

<Sample script={step5}></Sample>

- `@end` doesn't change what the player sees, but the warnings go away, and the story map marks the endings with a double outline.
- `{{restart text="Play again"}}` is a link that starts the story again from the beginning, once the player says they're sure. Anything in `{{double curly brackets}}` is worked out as the story runs, like the `{{#if}}` you used earlier. A published story also has a Restart button above it, but a link at the end is easier to find.

Open the map now to see the whole story: three endings, with the start at the top. When you play the story, the parts you've visited are outlined in green, so you can see which paths you haven't tested yet.

The finished story has three endings, an object to find, and a choice that depends on it. That's a complete Squiffy story.

## Set the mood

The story works, but you can make it feel more like being there. Make each part of the story fade in as it appears, the cave come slowly into focus, and the thing in the dark tremble:

<Sample alwaysShowRestart={true} script={step6}></Sample>

- `@transition fade 1s` makes text fade in over a second as it appears. There are others, such as `slide-up` and `zoom`: see [Transitions](/dynamic-text/#transitions).
- `[[]]:`, a section with no name, is the **master section**. What you put in it applies to every section and passage in the story, so here everything fades in. It can go anywhere in the file; the end is a good place, out of the way.
- The cave has a `@transition` of its own, `blur 2s`, which it uses instead: as the player goes in, it slowly comes into focus.
- `{{#style "shudder"}}...{{/style}}` makes the text inside it tremble, for as long as it's on the screen. There are more effects, such as `flicker`, `sway` and `blur`: see [Text effects](/dynamic-text/#text-effects).

A little of this goes a long way. Too many effects make a story harder to read, so save them for the moments that matter.

## Publish it

When you're happy with your story, it's time to share it. In the editor, open the **File** menu and choose **Publish…**, then pick how to share it:

- **textadventures.co.uk** puts your story online for free, where players can find it.
- **One HTML file** downloads your whole story as a single web page. Open it in a browser to play it, or send it to a friend.
- **Zip for a website** downloads the files to upload to a website of your own, or to [itch.io](https://itch.io/).

See [Publishing your story](/publishing/) for where to put it.

<Aside type="caution">
A story you start with **New story** in the web editor is kept in your browser, which can clear it. Use **File > Download project (.zip)** now and then to keep a copy, or use the [desktop app](/desktop-app/), which saves to a folder on your computer.
</Aside>

## Where next

Carry on with [Tutorial: make it your own](/tutorial-part-2/), where you'll give this story its own colours and font, a dark cave, the sound of the sea, and a panel showing what the player is carrying.

Here are some other ideas for taking your story further, and where to find out how:

- **Add more to the island.** Let the player find more things, and count them with [`@inc`](/attributes/#using-directives). Show different text the second time they visit somewhere, with [`{{visits}}`](/attributes/#the-visits-helper).
- **Ask the player's name**, and use it in the story: see [Player input](/player-input/).
- **Let the player look again.** Put [`@repeat`](/basic-concepts/#passages-you-can-click-again) in the footprints passage, and its link stays clickable.
- **Add more atmosphere** with [random text](/dynamic-text/#random), or text that types itself out with an [animation](/dynamic-text/#animation).
- **Borrow some patterns** from the [Cookbook](/cookbook/), such as an inventory, health, or a shop.
- **Play some complete stories**, and see how they work, in [Examples](/featured-examples/).

For everything else about writing stories, start with [Basic concepts](/basic-concepts/).
