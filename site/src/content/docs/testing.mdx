A story with choices has many ways through it, and it's easy to break one of them while working on another. Squiffy has several ways to test a story, from playing it as you write to tests that check every route still works.

| To | Use |
|---|---|
| Play the story as you write it | **Run** in the [editor](/editor/#testing-your-story), or `--serve` from the [command line](/cli/#http-server) |
| Test one scene without playing up to it | [Starting at a later section](#starting-at-a-later-section) |
| Find broken links, sections nobody can reach and dead ends | The editor's underlines and its problems list, or [`--check`](/cli/#checking-a-story) |
| Find problems on routes you wouldn't think to try | [`--random`](/cli/#playing-at-random) |
| Check a route still goes the same way after a change | [Playthrough tests](/cli/#testing-a-story) |

An [AI agent](/writing-with-ai/) can do all of these for you, too.

## Starting at a later section

To test a scene late in your story, you don't have to play up to it.

**In the editor**, the flag button above the preview lists your story's sections. Type to find one, and click it to run the story from there. **Run from this section** (Ctrl+Shift+Enter, or Cmd+Shift+Enter on Mac) does the same for the section the cursor is in. **Restart** starts at that section again, so you can try a scene as many times as you like.

**In a browser**, a story served by the command-line tool's [`--serve`](/cli/#http-server) has the same flag button above the story. You can also add `?start=` and the section's name to the address, which you can bookmark:

```text
http://localhost:8282/?start=the vault
```

To go back to playing from the beginning, choose the story's first section. Starting at a section replaces the game the browser had saved for the story.

When [playing from the command line](/cli/#other-options), use `--start`.

### Setting up a scene

Most late sections need some attributes to be set, such as the key the player picked up on the way. Write a section that sets them and then [goes to](/basic-concepts/#go-to-another-section) the scene, and mark it with `@test`:

```squiffy
[[vault with the key]]:
@test
@set has_key
@set score = 5
{{goto "the vault"}}
```

Start at that section, and you're in the vault with the key.

`@test` says the section is for testing, so nothing in the story needs to link to it. The editor and `--check` don't warn that it [can't be reached](/cli/#checking-a-story), and [`--random`](/cli/#playing-at-random) doesn't expect to visit it. The same goes for sections that only a `@test` section links to, so one `@test` section can be a list of links to others:

```squiffy
[[shortcuts]]:
@test
- [[The vault, with the key]](vault with the key)
- [[The vault, without it]](the vault)
- [[The last scene]](rooftop at night)
```

The flag button lists `@test` sections first. Like `@end`, `@test` doesn't change what the player sees.

In a browser, you can also set an attribute or two on the address, without writing a section. Add `&set=` for each, written as you'd write it after [`@set`](/attributes/#using-directives):

```text
http://localhost:8282/?start=the vault&set=has_key&set=score=5
```

### When you publish

Only the story as the editor's preview and `--serve` show it can start anywhere. The published story can't, so players can't skip ahead. Its `@test` sections are still in its files, with nothing linking to them, so don't put anything in one that a curious player mustn't find.

To publish a story that anyone can start anywhere, for other people to test, set `startAnywhere` in its [`squiffy.json`](/syntax/#squiffyjson):

```json
{
  "version": 1,
  "main": "story.squiffy",
  "startAnywhere": true
}
```

The published story then has the flag button, and takes `?start=` on its address. Take it out again before you publish the story for players.

## Finding problems

The editor [underlines problems](/editor/#writing-your-story) as you type, and the problems button in its toolbar lists them all: links to sections that don't exist, sections the player can't reach, dead ends, and mistakes in `{{...}}` text. From the command line, [`--check`](/cli/#checking-a-story) reports the same problems without building anything.

A warning that's wrong about your story can be [turned off](/cli/#turning-off-a-warning).

## Playing at random

[`--random`](/cli/#playing-at-random) plays your story many times, clicking links at random. It reports any errors and dead ends it finds, the endings it reached and the sections it never got to, and for each problem, the options that play the story back to it.

## Playthrough tests

A [playthrough test](/cli/#testing-a-story) is a way through your story that Squiffy has written down: the links clicked, the sections they went to and the attributes they changed. Record one with `--play --record`, and `--test` plays every test again, to check each still goes the same way after you've changed the story.
