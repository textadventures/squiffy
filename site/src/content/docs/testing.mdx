A story with choices has many ways through it, and it's easy to break one of them while working on another. Squiffy has several ways to test a story, from playing it as you write to tests that check every route still works.

| To | Use |
|---|---|
| Play the story as you write it | **Run** in the [editor](/editor/#testing-your-story), or `--serve` from the [command line](/cli/#http-server) |
| Test one scene without playing up to it | [Starting at a later section](#starting-at-a-later-section) |
| Find broken links, sections nobody can reach and dead ends | The editor's underlines and its problems list, or [`--check`](/cli/#checking-a-story) |
| Find problems on routes you wouldn't think to try | [`--random`](/cli/#playing-at-random) |
| Check a route still goes the same way after a change | [Playthrough tests](/cli/#testing-a-story) |

An [AI agent](/writing-with-ai/) can do all of these for you, too.

## Starting at a later section

To test a scene late in your story, you don't have to play up to it.

**In the editor**, the flag button above the preview lists your story's sections. Type to find one, and click it to run the story from there. **Run from this section** (Ctrl+Shift+Enter, or Cmd+Shift+Enter on Mac) does the same for the section the cursor is in. **Restart** starts at that section again, so you can try a scene as many times as you like. To play from the beginning again, choose **From the beginning** at the top of the list, or press **Run**.

<Screenshot name="start-at" alt="The preview's Start at menu, listing the story's sections, with a box to find one" />

**In a browser**, a story served by the command-line tool's [`--serve`](/cli/#http-server) has the same flag button above the story. You can also add `?start=` and the section's name to the address, which you can bookmark:

```text
http://localhost:8282/?start=the vault
```

To go back to playing from the beginning, choose **From the beginning** at the top of the list. Starting at a section replaces the game the browser had saved for the story.

When [playing from the command line](/cli/#other-options), use `--start`.

### Setting up a scene

Starting at a later section still sets what the start of your story sets with [`@set`](/attributes/#using-directives), so a line such as `@set loot = 0` at the top of the story works however it starts. The rest of the start, its text and any JavaScript, is skipped.

What's set on the way to the scene isn't, though, such as the key the player picked up, and most late sections need some attributes like that. Write a section that sets them and then [goes to](/basic-concepts/#go-to-another-section) the scene, and mark it with `@test`:

```squiffy
[[vault with the key]]:
@test
@set has_key
@set score = 5
{{goto "the vault"}}
```

Start at that section, and you're in the vault with the key.

When you're testing, in the editor's preview, VS Code's preview or a story served by `--serve`, an attribute shown with `{{name}}` that hasn't been set appears faintly as `{{name}}`, so you can see what a scene is missing. Players never see this: in the published story it shows nothing.

`@test` says the section is for testing, so nothing in the story needs to link to it. The editor and `--check` don't warn that it [can't be reached](/cli/#checking-a-story), and [`--random`](/cli/#playing-at-random) doesn't expect to visit it. The flag button lists `@test` sections first.

A `@test` section can link to others, so one can be a list of shortcuts:

```squiffy
[[shortcuts]]:
@test
- [[The vault, with the key]](vault with the key)
- [[The vault, without it]](the vault)
- [[The last scene]](rooftop at night)
```

Sections marked with `@test` are [left out of the story when it's published](#when-you-publish), so players never see them. That means the story itself mustn't lead to one. If a section that isn't for testing links to a `@test` section, embeds it or goes to it, the editor and `--check` warn you, as that won't work in the published story.

In a browser, you can also set an attribute or two on the address, without writing a section. Add `&set=` for each, written as you'd write it after [`@set`](/attributes/#using-directives):

```text
http://localhost:8282/?start=the vault&set=has_key&set=score=5
```

### When you publish

Only the story as the editor's preview and `--serve` show it can start anywhere. The published story can't, so players can't skip ahead, and it's published without its `@test` sections. That goes for the files `--serve` writes to the `build` folder too: it shows you the story for testing, but the files are the story for players.

Only the sections marked with `@test` are left out. A section that a `@test` section links to is still published unless it's marked too, so give each section that sets up a scene its own `@test`.

To publish a story that anyone can start anywhere, for other people to test, set `startAnywhere` in its [`squiffy.json`](/project-file/):

```json
{
  "version": 1,
  "main": "story.squiffy",
  "startAnywhere": true
}
```

The published story then has the flag button, takes `?start=` on its address, and keeps its `@test` sections. Take it out again before you publish the story for players.

## Finding problems

The editor [underlines problems](/editor/#writing-your-story) as you type, and the problems button in its toolbar lists them all: links to sections that don't exist, sections the player can't reach, dead ends, and mistakes in `{{...}}` text. From the command line, [`--check`](/cli/#checking-a-story) reports the same problems without building anything.

A warning that's wrong about your story can be [turned off](/cli/#turning-off-a-warning).

## Playing at random

[`--random`](/cli/#playing-at-random) plays your story many times, clicking links at random. It reports any errors and dead ends it finds, the endings it reached and the sections it never got to, and for each problem, the options that play the story back to it.

## Playthrough tests

A [playthrough test](/cli/#testing-a-story) is a way through your story that Squiffy has written down: the links clicked, the sections they went to and the attributes they changed. Record one with `--play --record`, and `--test` plays every test again, to check each still goes the same way after you've changed the story.
