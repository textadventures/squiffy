Squiffy creates HTML, CSS and JavaScript files, which you can upload anywhere that hosts web pages, such as [textadventures.co.uk](https://textadventures.co.uk/), [itch.io](https://itch.io/) or your own website. Players don't need to download or install anything. You don't need to share your `.squiffy` files: everything the game needs is in the HTML and JavaScript.

## Publishing from the editor

The **Share** button at the top of the editor, or **File > Share or publish…**, opens the Share or publish dialog, which has these ways to share your story:

<Screenshot name="publish" alt="The Share or publish dialog, with the story's title and description, and the ways to share it: a link, textadventures.co.uk, a zip for a website, one HTML file, or JavaScript only" />

- **A link** - Makes a link with your whole story in it, to send to anyone, with no account and nothing to upload: see [Sharing a link](#sharing-a-link). It's for a small story, or a sample of one.

- **textadventures.co.uk** - Puts your story on textadventures.co.uk, or updates it there: see [Publishing to textadventures.co.uk](#publishing-to-textadventurescouk).

- **Zip for a website** - Creates a ZIP file containing `index.html`, `style.css`, `story.js` and `squiffy.runtime.global.js`, along with your story's images and other files. Upload these files to any web server.

- **One HTML file** - Creates a single self-contained HTML file with all the CSS, JavaScript and images embedded. This is ideal for easy sharing, uploading to platforms that only accept one file, or offline distribution.

- **JavaScript only** - Creates just the `story.js` file, useful if you want to [embed your story in your own page](/embedding/).

Choose one and click **Publish** (or **Make the link**, for a link). The dialog opens on the one you chose last time, or on **A link** the first time.

At the top of the dialog is what your story's page will say it is, with a link to change it: see [Story details](#story-details). If your story has errors or warnings, the dialog says so, with a link to show them.

**File > Download project (.zip)** is different: it downloads your story's own files, for a backup or for editing elsewhere.

## Publishing to textadventures.co.uk

[textadventures.co.uk](https://textadventures.co.uk/) hosts games for free, and lists them for players to find. In the Share or publish dialog, **textadventures.co.uk** sends your story there:

1. The editor uploads your story, as the files in **Zip for a website**.
2. It gives you a link to continue on textadventures.co.uk, and opens it for you if your browser lets it. Log in there, or create an account.
3. The first time, fill in what players will see on your story's page. Its name, description, cover and language start as your [story details](#story-details), so it's worth setting those first.
4. After that, textadventures.co.uk knows the story, and shows one button to update it. Its page, reviews and link stay the same.

The editor says when your story is published, with a link to its page.

Nothing is published until you finish on textadventures.co.uk, and an upload you don't finish is deleted after an hour. A story can be up to 50 MB.

textadventures.co.uk knows a story by the `id` in its [`squiffy.json`](/project-file/), so a story without one is given one first. Keep that file with your story: a copy of the story with a different `id`, or none, would be published as a new game.

## Sharing a link

In the Share or publish dialog, **A link** turns your story into a web address, which you copy and send to anyone. Opening it plays the story on squiffystories.com, Squiffy's own site for playing stories:

<Screenshot name="publish-link" alt="The Share a link step of the Share or publish dialog, with the link to copy, an option to hide the Open in editor button, and how long the link is" />

```
https://squiffystories.com/v7/#story=1bY9Na4QwEIbv-RVDepX4LSIqCz0Vtj31Jh6s...
```

The story is in the link itself. Nothing is uploaded, so there's no account to make and nothing to host. That makes it a quick way to show someone a short story, a scene you'd like an opinion on, or an example of a problem you're asking for help with.

The player has a **Back** button (unless your story [turns it off](/buttons-panels-settings/#the-back-button)), a **Restart** button, and **Open in editor**, which gives whoever is playing their own copy of the story to read or change. Their copy is a new story, so nothing they do changes yours, or your link.

If you'd rather players didn't see **Open in editor**, tick **Hide the Open in editor button** under the link before you copy it. That only hides the button: the whole story is still in the link, so anyone who knows how can still read it, or open it in the editor themselves. A story with an error in it still shows the button, so you can open it to see what's wrong.

A link only has room for a small story:

- **Its text**: the story's `.squiffy`, `.css` and `.js` files, and its `squiffy.json`.
- **Small images**, of up to 4 KB each. An image takes up more of the link than the same amount of text does, so there's room for a few icons or small drawings, and SVG images take the least. The dialog lists any files it left out. A bigger image, or a sound, still works if your story uses its web address rather than a file.
- **Up to 32,000 characters** in all, which is about 60 KB of story text. The dialog won't make a longer link.

The dialog says how long the link is. Up to 2,000 characters, it can be pasted anywhere. Longer than that, it works in an email or as a link on a web page, but some chat apps cut a long message short, which breaks the link.

A few things are different from a story published the other ways:

- **The link is the story as it was when you made it.** If you change the story, make a new link. The old one goes on playing the old version.
- **A player's progress isn't saved.** Closing the page starts the story again, and there are no [saved games](/buttons-panels-settings/#saved-games).
- **Every link looks the same** when it's pasted into a chat or a social media post: its preview can't show the story's title or cover, because the site never sees which story it is.
- **The link says which version of Squiffy plays it** (the `v7`), so a story goes on working as it does now when later versions of Squiffy change things.

For a story that's finished, or one with pictures and sound, publish it [to textadventures.co.uk](#publishing-to-textadventurescouk) or [somewhere else](#where-to-put-it).

## Publishing from the command line

The [command-line tool](/cli/) writes the same files, and `--inline` and `--zip` make a single HTML file or a zip. See [Output formats](/cli/#output-formats).

## Story details

A published story's page says what the story is, for search results, for the preview a link to it shows on social media and in chat apps, and for sites that list stories, such as [textadventures.co.uk](https://textadventures.co.uk/). Its title is the main file's `@title`. Set the rest in the editor, with **Edit details** in the Publish dialog, or in your story's [`squiffy.json`](/project-file/):

<Screenshot name="story-details" alt="The Story details dialog, with the story's description, author, cover image and language" />

```json
{
  "$schema": "https://squiffystory.com/schema/squiffy.json",
  "version": 1,
  "id": "0b7c6d9e-3f1a-4e2b-9c5d-8a7f6e5d4c3b",
  "main": "story.squiffy",
  "description": "A short story about a cave.",
  "author": "Ann Author",
  "cover": "cover.png",
  "language": "en"
}
```

- `description` - What the story is about, in a sentence or two.
- `author` - Who wrote it.
- `cover` - An image for the story, by its path from the main file's folder, as the story would refer to it. It's published with the story, even if the story doesn't show it. A single HTML file leaves it out, as it has no files beside it.
- `language` - The language the story is written in, as a [language tag](https://www.w3.org/International/articles/language-tags/): `en` for English, `fr` for French, `pt-BR` for Brazilian Portuguese. Screen readers use it to pronounce the story's text, and browsers to offer translation and to hyphenate. A new story starts in the language your browser is in (in VS Code, VS Code's), and the editor offers that for a story that hasn't set one.

The page also gives the story's `id` as its [IFID](https://babel.ifarchive.org/), the number that identifies a piece of interactive fiction, which sites and tools that list stories use to tell which story it is, whatever it's called. So keep the `id` the same when you publish a new version.

All of these are optional. A story without a `squiffy.json` only has its title. In the editor, saving its details adds one.

## Where to put it

- **[textadventures.co.uk](https://textadventures.co.uk/)** lists games made with any system, and the editor can [publish straight to it](#publishing-to-textadventurescouk). See the [catalogue of Squiffy games](https://textadventures.co.uk/games/platform/squiffy) for what other people have made.
- **[itch.io](https://itch.io/)** can host HTML games, played in the browser: see [Publishing on itch.io](#publishing-on-itchio).
- **Your own website**: upload the files from the zip, or the single HTML file.

### Publishing on itch.io

1. In the editor, click **Share**, then **Zip for a website**, to download your story as a zip. Its `index.html` is at the top of the zip, which is where itch.io looks for it. From the command line, `npx @textadventures/squiffy-cli --zip` makes the same zip, as `build/output.zip`.
2. On itch.io, choose **Upload new project** from the menu under your name.
3. Set **Kind of project** to **HTML**.
4. Under **Uploads**, upload the zip, and tick **This file will be played in the browser**.
5. Under **Embed options**, set the **Viewport dimensions**. The game's frame is this size on the page, and the story scrolls inside it, so make it taller than itch.io's suggestion: 960 × 720, say. Tick **Mobile friendly** too, as a Squiffy story fits a phone's screen, and **Fullscreen button** if you like.
6. Fill in the rest of the page, such as the title, description and cover image, then save it, and look at it before you make it public.

To publish a new version, upload the new zip, tick **This file will be played in the browser** for it, and delete the old one.

## What players get

- **Their progress is saved** in their browser, so they can close the page and carry on where they left off when they come back. If you publish a changed version of your story at the same address, a returning player picks up from their saved game, so use **Restart** to see your changes.
- **Saved games.** The Save button keeps as many saved games as they like, and saves them to files and loads them back. See [Saved games](/buttons-panels-settings/#saved-games).
- **A Back button**, which undoes their choices a section or passage at a time. To leave it out, see [The Back button](/buttons-panels-settings/#the-back-button).
- **A Restart button**, and a button to switch between light and dark (see [Light and dark mode](/styling/#light-and-dark-mode)).
- **A mute button**, if the story has [music or sounds](/images-and-files/#music-and-sound).

To change how your story looks, see [Customising styles](/styling/).
