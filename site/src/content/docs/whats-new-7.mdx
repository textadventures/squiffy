Squiffy 7 brings a new editor. The language adds the [`@end`](/basic-concepts/#endings) directive for marking endings, [calculations](/helpers-reference/#calculations), and the same rules for `@set` as for `{{#if}}`. Squiffy 6 stories still work, and the editor converts them for you: see [Coming from Squiffy 6](#coming-from-squiffy-6). See [Using the editor](/editor/) for a tour.

## The new editor

- **Stories in several files.** A story can be split across `.squiffy` files, each in its own tab. New stories have a [`squiffy.json` project file](/basic-concepts/#stories-in-several-files), so every `.squiffy` file in the folder is part of the story, with no `@import`s needed. The command-line tool and the Vite plugin read it too.
- **Images and other files.** Add images, sounds and fonts to your story, and they're included when you publish it.
- **An editor that understands your story.** Problems such as links to missing sections, sections the player can't reach, and dead ends are underlined as you type, often with a quick fix. Links suggest your section and passage names as you type them. You can go from a link to its section, find every link to a section, and rename a section or passage everywhere at once.
- **A [story map](/editor/#the-story-map)** shows your sections, passages and links as a map you can move around, drawn from your script as you type. Problems show on it too, as does the way you went through the story when you test it. You can add links and sections, and rename them, from the map.
- **An outline** of your sections and passages, with a search box, replaces the section and passage dropdowns.
- **Run from any section**, so you can test the part you're working on without playing through from the start. As you click through the story, the script follows.
- **Stories in your browser**, without choosing a file, in every browser. Download one as a `.zip` to keep a copy.
- **Folders.** In Chrome and Edge, the editor can open a story's folder on your computer, and remembers the folders you've opened.
- **A dark theme**, and a text size setting. The preview follows it.
- **A [desktop app](/desktop-app/)** for Mac, Windows and Linux, with the same editor, which keeps your stories in folders on your computer.
- **Import from Twine.** Open a [Twine](https://twinery.org/) story, written in Harlowe or SugarCube, and the editor converts it to Squiffy. See [Coming from Twine](/coming-from-twine/).

## The language

- **Calculations.** Work out values with `+`, `-`, `*`, `/` and `%` in conditions, in `{{set}}`, `{{inc}}` and `{{dec}}`, and in the new `{{= ...}}`, which displays the result: `{{set "gold" gold + price * 2}}`, `{{#if gold >= price * 2}}`, or `That costs {{= price * 2}} gold.` There are matching [maths helpers](/helpers-reference/#maths-helpers) too: `add`, `sub`, `mul`, `div` and `mod`. See [Calculating values](/attributes/#calculating-values).
- **`@set` and link assignments work like `{{#if}}`.** Text goes in quotes and attributes don't need an `@`, so a value can be a calculation or a comparison: `@set weapon = "sword"`, `@set gold = gold + price * 2`, `@set rich = gold > 100`, or `[[Buy]](shop, gold -= price)`. See [Setting attributes](/attributes/#setting-attributes).
- **Go to another section from your text.** [`{{goto "section"}}`](/basic-concepts/#go-to-another-section) moves the story on without a link, for example `{{#if health <= 0}}{{goto "death"}}{{/if}}`, and `{{goto "darkness" after=3000}}` does it after a delay.
- **Visit and turn counts.** [`{{visits "cave"}}`](/attributes/#the-visits-helper) says how many times the player has been to a section or passage, for example `{{#if (visits "cave") > 2}}`, and the [`turns` attribute](/attributes/#counting-turns) counts the links clicked in the whole story.
- **Endings.** Mark the sections where your story ends with [`@end`](/basic-concepts/#endings), and the editor won't report them as dead ends.

## Published stories

- **Dark mode.** Published stories follow the reader's light or dark system setting, and have a button to switch between them. To keep your story light, see [Light and dark mode](/styling/#light-and-dark-mode).
- **A fresher look.** The default style has more space between lines, softer colours, a modern serif font, and text sizes that follow the reader's browser setting. Its colours are [CSS variables](/styling/#colours), so they're easier to change.

## Coming from Squiffy 6

If you had unsaved work in the Squiffy 6 editor, the home page offers to open it.

The Squiffy 6 editor opened and saved a single `.squiffy` file. To edit a story's file where it is, open the folder it's in. Otherwise, use **Open file** to copy it into your browser.

### Setting attributes

In Squiffy 6, text in `@set` and link assignments didn't need quotes, and another attribute was written with an `@`. A story with a [`squiffy.json`](/basic-concepts/#stories-in-several-files), which every story made in the Squiffy 7 editor has, uses the new rules:

| Squiffy 6 | Squiffy 7 |
|-----------|-----------|
| `@set weapon = sword` | `@set weapon = "sword"` |
| `@set best = @score` | `@set best = score` |
| `[[Go]](next, name = Bob, gold += @price)` | `[[Go]](next, name = "Bob", gold += price)` |

Numbers, `true` and `false`, `@set has_key`, `@set not has_key`, `:=`, `@inc` and `@dec` are the same in both.

A story without a `squiffy.json` keeps the Squiffy 6 rules, so it works unchanged. When a story gets a `squiffy.json`, its assignments are converted:

- **Editor:** opening a Squiffy 6 file in the browser converts it, and so does adding a `squiffy.json` to a folder (when you add a second file, or use the story map). Open files are changed in the editor, so you can use Undo.
- **CLI:** run with `--convert-v6`, which converts the story and adds a `squiffy.json`. See [Converting from Squiffy 6](/cli/#converting-from-squiffy-6).

Anything that can't be converted exactly, such as text with both kinds of quote in it, is left as it is and listed so you can change it by hand. In a story with a `squiffy.json`, the editor underlines assignments that still look like Squiffy 6, with a quick fix: an attribute written as `@score`, and text without quotes, such as `@set greeting = Hello there`, or `@set weapon = sword` when there's no attribute called `sword`.

### The `turns` attribute

Squiffy 7 counts the links the player clicks in an attribute called [`turns`](/attributes/#counting-turns). If your story keeps its own count called `turns`, for example with `@inc turns` in a master section, Squiffy now adds to it as well, so your count will go up twice as fast. You can take your own counting out and use Squiffy's, or rename your attribute. The editor underlines anywhere a story sets `turns`.

### Starting the story

In Squiffy 6, an `@import` above the main file's first section made the story begin in the imported file. Squiffy 7 begins in the main file. If your story relied on the old behaviour, choose the start section with [`@start`](/basic-concepts/#set-the-start-section).
