Squiffy 7 brings a new editor. The language adds the [`@end`](/basic-concepts/#endings) directive for marking endings, and [calculations](/helpers-reference/#calculations) in the text processor, but your Squiffy 6 stories work unchanged. See [Using the editor](/editor/) for a tour.

## The new editor

- **Stories in several files.** A story can be split across `.squiffy` files, each in its own tab. New stories have a [`squiffy.json` project file](/basic-concepts/#stories-in-several-files), so every `.squiffy` file in the folder is part of the story, with no `@import`s needed. The command-line tool and the Vite plugin read it too.
- **Images and other files.** Add images, sounds and fonts to your story, and they're included when you publish it.
- **An editor that understands your story.** Problems such as links to missing sections, sections the player can't reach, and dead ends are underlined as you type, often with a quick fix. Links suggest your section and passage names as you type them. You can go from a link to its section, find every link to a section, and rename a section or passage everywhere at once.
- **A [story map](/editor/#the-story-map)** shows your sections, passages and links as a map you can move around, drawn from your script as you type. Problems show on it too, as does the way you went through the story when you test it. You can add links and sections, and rename them, from the map.
- **An outline** of your sections and passages, with a search box, replaces the section and passage dropdowns.
- **Run from any section**, so you can test the part you're working on without playing through from the start. As you click through the story, the script follows.
- **Drafts** keep stories in your browser without choosing a file, in every browser. Download a draft as a `.zip` to keep a copy.
- **Folders.** In Chrome and Edge, the editor can work on a story's files where they are on your computer, and remembers the folders you've opened.
- **A dark theme**, and a text size setting. The preview follows it.
- **A [desktop app](/desktop-app/)** for Mac, Windows and Linux, with the same editor, which keeps your stories in folders on your computer.

## The language

- **Calculations.** Work out values with `+`, `-`, `*`, `/` and `%` in conditions, in `{{set}}`, `{{inc}}` and `{{dec}}`, and in the new `{{= ...}}`, which displays the result: `{{set "gold" gold + price * 2}}`, `{{#if gold >= price * 2}}`, or `That costs {{= price * 2}} gold.` There are matching [maths helpers](/helpers-reference/#maths-helpers) too: `add`, `sub`, `mul`, `div` and `mod`. See [Calculating values](/attributes/#calculating-values).
- **Endings.** Mark the sections where your story ends with [`@end`](/basic-concepts/#endings), and the editor won't report them as dead ends.

## Published stories

- **Dark mode.** Published stories follow the reader's light or dark system setting, and have a button to switch between them. To keep your story light, see [Light and dark mode](/styling/#light-and-dark-mode).
- **A fresher look.** The default style has more space between lines, softer colours, a modern serif font, and text sizes that follow the reader's browser setting. Its colours are [CSS variables](/styling/#colours), so they're easier to change.

## Coming from Squiffy 6

If you had unsaved work in the Squiffy 6 editor, the home page offers to open it as a draft.

The Squiffy 6 editor opened and saved a single `.squiffy` file. To edit a story's file where it is, open the folder it's in. Otherwise, use **Open file** to copy it into a draft.

In Squiffy 6, an `@import` above the main file's first section made the story begin in the imported file. Squiffy 7 begins in the main file. If your story relied on the old behaviour, choose the start section with [`@start`](/basic-concepts/#set-the-start-section).
