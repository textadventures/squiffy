Squiffy 7 brings a new editor. The language is the same as in Squiffy 6, so your stories work unchanged, apart from [one fix](#where-the-story-starts) to where a story starts. See [Using the editor](/editor/) for a tour.

## The new editor

- **Stories in several files.** A story can be split across `.squiffy` files, which the main file `@import`s, each in its own tab.
- **Images and other files.** Add images, sounds and fonts to your story, and they're included when you publish it.
- **An editor that understands your story.** Problems such as links to missing sections are underlined as you type, often with a quick fix. Links suggest your section and passage names as you type them. You can go from a link to its section, find every link to a section, and rename a section or passage everywhere at once.
- **An outline** of your sections and passages, with a search box, replaces the section and passage dropdowns.
- **Run from any section**, so you can test the part you're working on without playing through from the start. As you click through the story, the script follows.
- **Drafts** keep stories in your browser without choosing a file, in every browser. Download a draft as a `.zip` to keep a copy.
- **Folders.** In Chrome and Edge, the editor can work on a story's files where they are on your computer, and remembers the folders you've opened.
- **A dark theme**, and a text size setting.

## Coming from Squiffy 6

If you had unsaved work in the Squiffy 6 editor, the home page offers to open it as a draft.

The Squiffy 6 editor opened and saved a single `.squiffy` file. To edit a story's file where it is, open the folder it's in. Otherwise, use **Open file** to copy it into a draft.

### Where the story starts

A story begins in the main file's first section, unless you choose another with [`@start`](/basic-concepts/#set-the-start-section). In Squiffy 6, an `@import` above that section made the story begin in the imported file instead. Squiffy 7 begins in the main file, wherever the `@import` is. If your story relied on starting in an imported file, add `@start` with that section's name.
